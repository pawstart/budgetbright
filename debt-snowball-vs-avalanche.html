Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche: Which One Is Right for You?

This is general education, not personal financial advice.

Both methods start the same way: list every debt, keep paying the minimum on all of them, and put any extra money toward one debt at a time. The difference is which debt you attack first.

The snowball method

Pay the smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate. When that debt is gone, roll its payment into the next smallest. The win is psychological: you clear debts faster, see progress, and stay motivated.

The avalanche method

Pay the highest interest rate first, regardless of balance. When that debt is gone, roll its payment into the next highest rate. The win is mathematical: in general, you pay less interest overall if you stick with it.

Illustrative comparison

Example only, with three debts: $500, $2,000, and $5,000. Snowball attacks $500 first. Avalanche attacks whichever of the three has the highest interest rate first, even if that is the $5,000 debt. Neither method is “wrong” — they optimise for different things: motivation vs. interest saved.

How to choose

  • Choose snowball if you have struggled to stick with plans before and need early wins.
  • Choose avalanche if you are motivated by numbers and will stick with a longer first payoff.
  • Choose the one you will actually finish. A perfect plan you abandon helps no one.

What not to do

Do not miss minimum payments, do not add new debt while paying old debt down if you can avoid it, and do not ignore a debt because it feels stressful — list it, even if the number is uncomfortable.

FAQ

Can I switch methods later? Yes. You can start with one and switch if motivation or circumstances change.

Do I need a tracker? A simple list or printable tracker helps you see progress. That is covered in the Debt Payoff Tracker article in the roadmap.
