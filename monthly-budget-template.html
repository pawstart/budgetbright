Free Monthly Budget Template: How to Use It Step by Step

This is general education, not personal financial advice.

A monthly budget template is a single page where you write your income, your planned spending, and your actual spending for the month. It does not need to be fancy. It needs to be used.

The free BudgetBright Monthly Budget Template is part of the free printable pack at launch. The template file itself is to be finalised at launch — this article explains exactly how to use it, so you can start with paper today if you want to.

What the template includes

  • Income: take-home pay, and any other regular income.
  • Fixed costs: housing, utilities, phone, transport, minimum debt payments, insurance.
  • Variable costs: food, household, clothes, fun, eating out.
  • Savings: emergency fund, sinking funds, and any other saving pots.
  • Debt: extra payments beyond minimums, if you make any.
  • Totals: planned total, actual total, and the difference.

How to fill it in, row by row

Step 1: Income

Write your take-home pay for the month. If pay varies, use a lower recent month as a cautious starting point, as explained in How to Make a Budget for Beginners.

Step 2: Fixed costs

Write each fixed cost and its planned amount. These are the easiest to fill in because they rarely change month to month.

Step 3: Variable costs

Write a planned amount for each variable category. Use last month's actual spending as a starting point if you have it; otherwise, write a realistic first guess and adjust next month.

Step 4: Savings and debt

Write planned savings first if you can, even a small amount, then extra debt payments. See Emergency Fund and Sinking Funds for those pots.

Step 5: Totals

Add up planned income, planned spending, and planned savings. If spending plus savings is more than income, adjust a variable category before the month starts — that is the point of planning in advance.

How to review it at month-end

At month-end, fill in the actual column, compare planned vs. actual by category, and note one change for next month. That review is also step 2 of the Monthly Money Reset. Do not rewrite the whole template — just adjust the categories that drifted.

FAQ

Paper or spreadsheet? Either works. Paper is simple and visible; a spreadsheet adds up totals for you. Use whichever you will actually open.

What if I go over in one category? Note it, adjust next month, and move on. One over-spend does not cancel the budget.

Do I need a new template every month? Yes, one per month, so you can compare months and see patterns over time.
