Debt Payoff Tracker: Free Printable + How to Use It

This is general education, not personal financial advice.

A debt payoff tracker is one place — one page per debt, or one page for all your debts — where you record each balance, each minimum payment, each extra payment, and the remaining balance over time. It does not pay the debt for you. It makes progress visible, month by month, so you can see balances falling instead of only feeling them.

Step 1: List every debt

List every debt on the tracker, using general category labels from your own statements — for example, a card balance or a loan, as categories, with no specific lender or product named or recommended here. For each debt, record:

  • Debt name in your own words: a label you recognise, such as “Card A” or “Loan B”.
  • Starting balance: the balance on the day you start tracking, from your own statement.
  • Minimum payment: the minimum amount due each month, from your own statement.
  • Rate band in your own records: the rate shown on your own statement, recorded for your own reference. Do not publish personal figures anywhere.

List all of them, even the small ones and the ones you would rather forget. A debt hidden from the tracker is a debt the tracker cannot help you plan for.

Step 2: Choose an order

The tracker works with either common order, explained in general terms in Debt Snowball vs. Debt Avalanche:

  • Smallest balance first (snowball): clear the smallest balance first for the motivation of a win, then roll that payment to the next debt.
  • Highest rate first (avalanche): clear the highest-rate debt first, in general terms, for the interest saved over time.

Pick one order and write it at the top of the tracker. Changing order halfway through makes the tracker hard to read. Either order works with this sheet — the sheet records payments and balances, it does not choose for you.

Step 3: Update it monthly

Once a month, on the same day if possible, update every row:

  1. Record the payment made that month, split into minimum payment and extra payment if you made one.
  2. Record the new balance from your statement.
  3. Record the total paid so far on that debt.
  4. Colour in the progress bar for that debt, up to the share paid off.

When a debt reaches zero, mark it cleared, celebrate it, and roll its payment — minimum plus any extra you were paying — to the next debt in your chosen order. That roll-over is how a tracker turns one cleared debt into faster progress on the next.

What not to do

  • Do not miss minimum payments while tracking — the tracker records minimums first, extras second, for a reason.
  • Do not add new debt while tracking old debt if avoidable — new balances undo the progress the tracker is showing.
  • Do not hide a debt from the tracker — the tracker only works on debts it can see.
  • Do not treat the tracker as advice — it is a recording tool. Which order or payment plan suits you is a personal decision.

When to seek help

If minimum payments cannot be met, or debt feels unmanageable, consider speaking to a qualified adviser or a local support service early, rather than waiting. This article does not offer personal debt advice, and it does not replace professional help for a difficult debt situation.

FAQ

Should I include all debts? Yes. Include every debt you owe, even small ones, so the tracker shows the full picture and the roll-over order is correct.

How often do I update it? Monthly, after payments have cleared and you have your new statement balances. Updating more often is fine, but monthly is the habit that lasts.

What if a balance goes up? That happens when interest, charges, or new spending outweigh payments. Record the real balance, note why it rose if you know, and carry on — a tracker that only records good months is not a tracker.

Can I use it with snowball and avalanche? Yes. The sheet records balances and payments either way; the order you write at the top is the only part that changes.
